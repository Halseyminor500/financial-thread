## 0. THE ANSWERS, IN PLAIN WORDS

### 0.1 "Thou shalt" — yes. The duty is written in "shall"; only the extension is written in "may".

- Family Code § 12, the Code's own rule of construction (verified at source today; `LAW/VERIFIED_AT_SOURCE_2026-11-01_FAM12_721_1100_1101_2030_CCP12_583.210_and_disclosure_caselaw.md` Part A.2): "“Shall” is mandatory and “may” is permissive. “Shall not” and “may not” are prohibitory." Section 6 of the same Part: "Unless the provision or context otherwise requires, the general provisions and rules of construction in this part govern the construction of this code." The Court of Appeal has applied § 12 to this very chapter: In re Marriage of Evans (2014) 229 Cal.App.4th 374, 383, quoting § 2107(c) "the court shall … impose money sanctions" and adding "(See § 12 [“[s]hall” is mandatory].)" (law file Part B.10).
- The history of the 60-day sentence (law file Part A.16, from the chaptered bills): before Stats. 2012, ch. 107 (AB 1406) the statute required the declaration "after or concurrently with service of the petition for dissolution"; AB 1406's Legislative Counsel's Digest says "This bill instead would require the petitioner to serve the preliminary declaration of disclosure either concurrently with the petition for dissolution or within 60 days of filing the petition". Since 2012 the petitioner's clock runs from the filing of the petition, not from its service.
- Every operative sentence of the duty uses "shall": § 2103 "each party … shall serve on the other party a preliminary declaration of disclosure under Section 2104 … and shall file proof of service of each with the court." § 2104(a) "each party shall serve on the other party a preliminary declaration of disclosure, executed under penalty of perjury". § 2104(f) "The petitioner shall serve the other party with the preliminary declaration of disclosure either concurrently with the petition for dissolution or legal separation, or within 60 days of filing the petition."
- The one place the parties get a "may" is the extension, and it is fenced: "The time periods specified in this subdivision may be extended by written agreement of the parties or by court order." (§ 2104(f)). Nothing else in §§ 2100–2113 lets a party move the date or skip the preliminary declaration by agreement (timing file, Part 15(3): "the text is silent" on any stipulated waiver of the preliminary declaration).
- The court's remedy words are also "shall": § 2107(c) "the court shall, in addition to any other remedy provided by law, impose money sanctions against the noncomplying party. Sanctions shall be in an amount sufficient to deter repetition of the conduct"; § 2107(d) "if a court enters a judgment when the parties have failed to comply with all disclosure requirements of this chapter, the court shall set aside the judgment. The failure to comply with the disclosure requirements does not constitute harmless error."
- The "may" words belong to the injured party's choices and the court's discretion: § 2107(a) the complying party "may, within a reasonable time, request preparation"; § 2107(b) "may do one or more of the following" (compel; evidence preclusion; court-granted waiver); § 2104(d) a declarant "may amend"; § 2107(e) the court "may order" the declarations produced on a set-aside motion.
- Shall/may count on the verified text of §§ 2100–2110 (Part 2 below): "shall" 27 times, "may" 17 times. In §§ 2103, 2104 and 2107 alone: "shall" 19 times, "may" 10 times, of which three are "may have an interest / may be liable", two describe what the court may do, and five are the parties' options (amend; state a characterization; extend the time in writing or by order; the complying party's request and motions). No "may" lets a party skip or delay the preliminary declaration alone.

### 0.2 For whose benefit — the Legislature says the State's, in those words.

- § 2100(a): "It is the policy of the State of California (1) to marshal, preserve, and protect community and quasi-community assets and liabilities that exist at the date of separation so as to avoid dissipation of the community estate before distribution, (2) to ensure fair and sufficient child and spousal support awards, and (3) to achieve a division of community and quasi-community assets and liabilities … as provided under California law."
- § 2100(b): "Sound public policy further favors the reduction of the adversarial nature of marital dissolution and the attendant costs by fostering full disclosure and cooperative discovery."
- § 2100(c): "a full and accurate disclosure of all assets and liabilities in which one or both parties have or may have an interest must be made in the early stages of a proceeding for dissolution of marriage … regardless of the characterization as community or separate, together with a disclosure of all income and expenses of the parties."
- § 2120(a): "The State of California has a strong policy of ensuring the division of community and quasi-community property … These policy goals can only be implemented with full disclosure of community, quasi-community, and separate assets, liabilities, income, and expenses … and decisions freely and knowingly made."
- The court's own order, in the packet the firm sent him on 1/25/2024 (LASC FAM 111, dated 1/16/2024, signed "Shelley Kaufman, Supervising Judge, Family Law Division"; g:18d424f91222fab9 attachment 07; read by me today): "The court orders that both spouses in this case must: Comply with the duty to share financial information." / "Duty to Exchange Financial Information — Both parties have a duty to disclose all material information. ### 0.6 Is it equitable? — The Code answers in its own words, and the FAM 111 order answers in one sentence.

- § 2100(a)(2): the policy is "to ensure fair and sufficient child and spousal support awards".
- § 2120(b): "It occasionally happens that the division of property or the award of support, whether made as a result of agreement or trial, is inequitable when made due to the nondisclosure or other misconduct of one of the parties." § 2120(c): "The public policy of assuring finality of judgments must be balanced against the public interest in ensuring proper division of marital property, in ensuring sufficient support awards, and in deterring misconduct."
- § 2102(a): "From the date of separation to the date of the distribution of the community or quasi-community asset or liability in question, each party is subject to the standards provided in Section 721, as to all activities that affect the assets and liabilities of the other party, including … (1) The accurate and complete disclosure of all assets and liabilities in which the party has or may have an interest or obligation and all current earnings, accumulations, and expenses".
- § 721(b) (law file Part A.3): "in transactions between themselves, spouses are subject to the general rules governing fiduciary relationships that control the actions of persons occupying confidential relations with each other. This confidential relationship imposes a duty of the highest good faith and fair dealing on each spouse, and neither shall take any unfair advantage of the other." It includes "(1) Providing each spouse access at all times to any books kept regarding a transaction for the purposes of inspection and copying. (2) Rendering upon request, true and full information of all things affecting any transaction that concerns the community property." § 2102(a) carries these standards "From the date of separation to the date of the distribution".
- § 1100(e) (law file Part A.4): "Each spouse shall act with respect to the other spouse in the management and control of the community assets and liabilities in accordance with the general rules governing fiduciary relationships … until such time as the assets and liabilities have been divided by the parties or by a court. This duty includes the obligation to make full disclosure to the other spouse of all material facts and information regarding the existence, characterization, and valuation of all assets in which the community has or may have an interest and debts for which the community is or may be liable, and to provide equal access to all information, records, and books that pertain to the value and character of those assets and debts, upon request." The Court of Appeal: the § 721 duties "are made specifically applicable during dissolution proceedings by section 1100, subdivision (e)" (In re Marriage of Feldman (2007) 153 Cal.App.4th 1470, 1475).
- § 2030(a)(1) (law file Part A.6): "the court shall ensure that each party has access to legal representation, including access early in the proceedings, to preserve each party's rights by ordering, if necessary based on the income and needs assessments, one party … to pay to the other party … whatever amount is reasonably necessary for attorney's fees and for the cost of maintaining or defending the proceeding during the pendency of the proceeding." § 2030(a)(2): "A party who lacks the financial ability to hire an attorney may request, as an in pro per litigant, that the court order the other party, if that other party has the financial ability, to pay a reasonable amount to allow the unrepresented party to retain an attorney in a timely manner before proceedings in the matter go forward." § 2032(b): the court weighs "the need for the award to enable each party, to the extent practical, to have sufficient financial resources to present the party's case adequately" and apportions "the overall cost of the litigation equitably between the parties under their relative circumstances." The 4/21/2026 order records your allegation that support payments have "not allowed him to hire counsel or funding for forensic work"; the register shows no filing by you titled as a request for attorney's fees under § 2030; whether your 2025 ex parte "Emergency Orders for Support" requests asked for fees is not shown by the register entries read (Q-H44).
- The FAM 111 order: "If you have greater access to certain information than the other spouse, you must provide such information to the other spouse." and "You must provide access to all information, records and books that pertain to the value and character of assets and debts in which the community has or may have an interest."
- The limit, also in the Code: § 2123: "a judgment may not be set aside simply because the court finds that it was inequitable when made". And § 2121(b): before granting relief "the court shall find that the facts alleged as the grounds for relief materially affected the original outcome and that the moving party would materially benefit from the granting of the relief."
- So: the Code names the imbalance you describe as the thing it is written to prevent (§ 2100, § 2120(b)), gives the duty to the spouse with "greater access" (FAM 111) and makes the disclosure "regardless of the characterization as community or separate" (§ 2100(c); § 2104(c)(1)). It does not give a remedy for inequity as such; it gives remedies for noncompliance (§ 2107), for perjury and nondisclosure (§ 2122(b), (f)), and for breach of the § 721 duty (§ 1101(g), law file Part A.5: remedies for breach of the fiduciary duty "shall include, but not be limited to, an award to the other spouse of 50 percent, or an amount equal to 50 percent, of any asset undisclosed or transferred in breach of the fiduciary duty plus attorney's fees and court costs"; § 1101(h): 100 percent "when the breach falls within the ambit of Section 3294 of the Civil Code"; In re Marriage of Simmons (2013) 215 Cal.App.4th 584, 587: § 1101(h) "applies only to the nondisclosure of a community property asset").

### 0.7 "A nonnegotiable disclosure that is a predicate of service" — half right, and the half that is wrong matters.

- Nonnegotiable: yes, as between the parties. The preliminary declaration cannot be waived by stipulation; only its date can be moved, in writing or by order (§ 2104(f)); it can be excused only by the court on the complying party's motion (§ 2107(b)(3)) or in a default case served by publication or posting (§ 2110). Even the waiver of the final declaration requires both parties to swear that "the preliminary declarations of disclosure have been completed and exchanged" (§ 2105(d)(1)).
- A predicate of service: no. The words allow the petitioner to serve the declaration "either concurrently with the petition … or within 60 days of filing the petition" (§ 2104(f)). A petition may be served without it; yours was (1/25/2024 sent; 3/8/2024 acknowledged). Service of the summons is governed by the Code of Civil Procedure (CCP 415.30 for the acknowledgment route), and that is what gives the court jurisdiction over the respondent (FL-180 item 3: "The court acquired jurisdiction of the respondent on (date): 3/8/24").
- What the preliminary declaration IS a predicate of, in the words: judgment and agreement. § 2100(c): the disclosure exists "so that at the time the parties enter into an agreement for the resolution of any of these issues, or at the time of trial on these issues, each party will have a full and complete knowledge of the relevant underlying facts." § 2107(d): a judgment entered "when the parties have failed to comply with all disclosure requirements of this chapter" is one the court "shall set aside". § 2106: no judgment on property rights without the final declarations (or their waiver). § 2108: no court-ordered liquidation "unless … the appropriate declaration of disclosure has been served by the moving party." § 2110: even in a default, "a preliminary declaration of disclosure by the petitioner is required".

### 0.8 What the cases add, and where they cut against your reading (all read at source on CourtListener; law file Part B)

- **Sanctions need no showing of harm.** Feldman, 153 Cal.App.4th at 1479–1480: "Neither statute sets forth any requirement of separate injury to the complaining spouse as a precondition to the imposition of sanctions." … "Because there is no requirement that Elena show harm as a prerequisite to an award of sanctions, we need not and do not analyze whether Elena was harmed". At 1481: the statute does not require that a party first "seek further disclosure" and "bring a motion to either compel further responses or preclude evidence."
- **Only a "complying party" may use § 2107.** In re Marriage of Fong (2011) 193 Cal.App.4th 278, 289: "only a “complying party,” meaning a party that has served a preliminary or final declaration of disclosure, is entitled to seek an award of monetary sanctions against the other party". Elden at 1510: "In order for Wife to invoke the remedies of section 2107, Wife must first become a “complying party.”" You served your preliminary declaration on 7/2/2024 (your FL-141). The final declarations were waived on both sides by the FL-144 stipulation. Whether a party whose final declaration was waived by stipulation counts as "complying" for § 2107(c) is not answered by any opinion read: Fong's movant had neither served nor waived a final declaration. § 271 is different: "Unlike section 2107, subdivision (c), section 271 does not necessarily require that the moving party be in compliance with particular obligations before moving for an award." (Fong at 293.)
- **Setting a judgment aside needs a showing that the nondisclosure mattered.** In re Marriage of Steiner & Hosseini (2004) 117 Cal.App.4th 519, 528: "a noncomplying litigant must identify some portion of the judgment materially affected by the nondisclosure"; the "not harmless error" sentence "must give way to the Constitution and the balance of the legislative scheme." In re Marriage of Kieturakis (2006) 138 Cal.App.4th 56, 92: "a showing of prejudice is still required despite this statutory language". § 2121(b) says the same in the statute.
- **Lateness alone.** No published opinion was located holding that a preliminary declaration served after day 60 is void, or that lateness alone compels sanctions or a set-aside (law file Part C.3). Elden at 1510, on a final declaration served late: "We do not perceive what prejudice Wife suffered by Husband's dilatory filing." In Fong the petitioner "served her preliminary declaration of disclosure three years later" (at 282) and the court decided her sanctions appeal on another ground (fn. 4). In re Marriage of Campi (2013) 212 Cal.App.4th 1565, 1576: a husband whose own preliminary declaration came three years late was held to "invited error from which he is estopped."
- **The duty weighs more on the spouse who holds the records.** In re Marriage of Brewer & Federici (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 1334, 1348: "a spouse who is in a superior position to obtain records or information from which an asset can be valued and can reasonably do so must acquire and disclose such information to the other spouse." Fong at 293: "As the party in possession of a greater share of the community assets, Gary bore a greater burden of disclosure and fiduciary responsibility with respect to the community assets." In re Marriage of Rossi (2001) 90 Cal.App.4th 34, 42: "full disclosure of marital assets is absolutely essential to the trial court in determining the proper dissolution of property and resolving support issues." Feldman at 1488: a spouse "had a fiduciary duty to disclose the existence of the 401(k) account on the Schedule in the first place without prodding from Elena and to produce relevant documents upon Elena's request."
- **What this means for the three bodies, in words only.** The court is the body the statute names for every § 2107 remedy, and it has ruled twice that the declaration was served.
