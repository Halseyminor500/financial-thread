> "Complaints are forwarded to investigation if they set out plausible, nonconclusory factual allegations that: (a) if
> true, would constitute a violation of the State Bar Act or Rules of Professional Conduct; or (b) suggest reasonable

### 1.4 The deferral rule's words

- **Rule of Procedure 5.21(C)(3), with its lead-in:** "(C) Tolling. The time limit for the filing of an initial pleading under
  section (A) above is tolled: … (3) while civil, criminal, or administrative investigations or proceedings based on the same
  acts or circumstances as the violation, including but not limited to proceedings seeking to determine whether a
  violation has occurred, the scope of the violation, the harm resulting from the violation, or any actions to be taken to
  remediate the violation, including efforts to collect funds owed as the result of the violation, are pending with any
  governmental agency, court, or tribunal;"
- **Rule 5.21(A):** "…the initial pleading must be filed within the later of (1) five years from the date the violation
  occurred or (2) two years from the date the first complaint regarding the violation is submitted to the State Bar, so long
  as that complaint is submitted to the State Bar within five years from the date the violation occurred."
- **Rule 5.21(B):** "A violation … occurs when every element of a violation has occurred."
- **Rule 5.50(A):** "…the Court may abate any proceeding in whole or in part. Abatement stays the proceeding in the State Bar
  Court and tolls all time limitations in the proceeding…"
- **SB 211 report, printed p. 32:** "OCTC currently defers further review and investigation of some complaints based on pending
  criminal or civil litigation involving similar material allegations. Under State Bar Rule of Procedure 5.21(C)(3),
  deferral based on pending criminal or civil litigation tolls the five-year time limit for filing an NDC."
- **SB 211 report, footnote 16:** "As noted, the State Bar Rules of Procedure implicitly recognize OCTC's ability to defer,
  with deferral tolling the otherwise applicable time limit for filing."
- **SB 211 report, printed p. 8:** "…provides OCTC with the necessary prosecutorial discretion to await a resolution of issues by
  a civil or criminal court when there is a substantial overlap with issues posed by a disciplinary complaint."

  the decisions of the courts" appear on no calbar.ca.gov page. They appear in D2 and in one unauthenticated OCTC letter
  hosted by a third party (LAW/…jurisdiction_and_intake §4).

### 1.5 What "plausible, nonconclusory factual allegations" means here

Each allegation must be built from four things: **a date, an actor, an act, and the document that shows the act.**
- **The actor** is the person whose name, signature or address is on the document. For this file, the actor is either
  Lance S. Spiegel personally (SBN 51625), or a named nonlawyer on his staff (Anna Kralle/Vanmali, "Paralegal";
  Matthew Sirriyeh, "Paralegal and Notary Public").
  - A staff member's act reaches Spiegel only through rule 5.3 (or rule 8.4(a), "through the acts of another"), and only
    on the facts 5.3 requires.
- **The act** is described in the document's own words: what was filed, signed, sent or said, and where.
- **The document** is a COURT, SWORN, CORRESPONDENCE or third-party record identified by an SP-id that a reader has read.
  - An ACCOUNT can point to a document. It cannot stand in for one.
  - AI material can do neither.
- **"Nonconclusory"** means that the allegation states what the document says and what the other document says, side by
  side. It does not say "lied", "fraud", "false" or "perjury".
- **"Plausible"** is the Bar's own gate on the inference. Where an element is a state of mind, the allegation must name the
  circumstance from which the Bar could infer it. For example: the correct date appears in the declarant's own exhibit; the
  firm's own email gives a different reason; the firm's own filing cites the document said to be missing.
- An allegation built from a date, a name and a document that does not, on its face, bear on the element is the Bar's
  "conclusory or speculative" kind. So is an allegation built only from a pattern, or from a count with no list behind it.
  The Bar's 2026 letter used that word about the email-account allegation: "you appear to rely solely on speculation".

### 3.0 Rule key — each candidate rule's elements, in its own words, and the mental state the words require

Element numbers (E1, E2 …) are used in the cells below. "1.0.1(f)" means "'Knowingly,' 'known,' or 'knows' means actual
knowledge of the fact in question. A person's knowledge may be inferred from circumstances." "1.0.1(d)" means "'Fraud' or
'fraudulent' means conduct that is fraudulent under the law of the applicable jurisdiction and has a purpose to deceive."
Comment [3] to 1.0.1 adds that fraud "does not include merely negligent misrepresentation".

### 7.1 BPC § 6043.5, the Bar's form, and what follows

**§ 6043.5, verbatim:** "(a) Every person who reports to the State Bar or causes a complaint to be filed with the State Bar
that an attorney has engaged in professional misconduct, knowing the report or complaint to be false and malicious, is
guilty of a misdemeanor. (b) The State Bar may, in its discretion, notify the appropriate district attorney or city attorney
that a person has filed what the State Bar believes to be a false and malicious report or complaint against an attorney and
recommend prosecution of the person under subdivision (a)."

**The form's certification:** "By checking this box I certify that all information on this form is true and correct."

**The online acknowledgment:** "I understand that every person who reports to the State Bar or causes a complaint to be filed
with the State Bar that an attorney has engaged in professional misconduct, knowing the report or complaint to be false and
malicious, is guilty of a misdemeanor."

**Disclosure to the lawyer:**
- "during the investigation the State Bar will be required to disclose to the lawyer the facts and circumstances being
  investigated."
- "The accused attorney is usually informed and can respond."

  documented, with account labelled as account, is the opposite of what § 6043.5 describes.

### 7.2 CRPC 3.10 applies to lawyers only

Rule 3.10(a) begins "A lawyer shall not threaten…". Its actor is a lawyer. **Halsey is not a lawyer, and rule 3.10 does not
govern what he writes.** What governs a non-lawyer's writing that pairs accusation with a request is the Penal Code (7.3).
The rule's comment is still a useful line to remember:
- **Comment [1]:** "a lawyer could not state or imply that a criminal or administrative action will be pursued unless the
  opposing party agrees to settle the civil dispute".
- **Comment [2]:** the rule "does not prohibit actually presenting criminal, administrative or disciplinary charges".

### 7.3 Penal Code §§ 519, 523 and 524 (LAW/…PC_guardrails_extortion)

- **§ 519, verbatim:** "Fear, such as will constitute extortion, may be induced by a threat of any of the following: 1. To
