### (i) What the institution requires before acting

- "The State Bar’s Office of Chief Trial Counsel (OCTC) has reviewed your complaint against Lance Stephen Spiegel to determine whether there are sufficient grounds to prosecute a possible violation of the State Bar Act and/or Rules of Professional Conduct."
- "Based on our evaluation of the information provided, we are closing your complaint."
- "It is misconduct for a lawyer to fail to support the Constitution and laws of the United States and this state, including those governing discovery and service of process. Similarly, it is misconduct for a lawyer to seek to mislead a court by artifice or false statement of fact or law. Likewise, it is misconduct for a lawyer to engage in conduct involving dishonesty, fraud, or deceit."
- **"However, to investigate allegations of lawyer misconduct, the State Bar needs specific facts which, if proved, would establish a violation of the lawyer’s ethical duties under the Rules of Professional Conduct or the State Bar Act. Conclusory or speculative allegations unsupported by allegations of specific facts are insufficient to warrant investigation."**
- "Although you take the position that the lawyer’s conduct amounted to an ethical violation, there are insufficient facts presented to show that the lawyer willfully violated the law or otherwise committed misconduct. Specifically, you have failed to offer any specific facts to support your contention that the lawyer had committed an unlawful act with respect to your email account and you appear to rely solely on speculation. As discussed above, conclusions unsupported by allegations of specific facts are insufficient to warrant investigation."
- "In addition, the lawyer has a duty to act in their client’s best interest and they are entitled to rely on information from their client in taking the actions they deem necessary to protect those interests, even when those actions conflict with your goals."
- **"Further, a disagreement between opposing litigants with respect to discovery or any challenges to service or a party’s declaration does not mean that the lawyer committed misconduct and these are matters best resolved by the court having jurisdiction over the case. If that court were to find any impropriety by the lawyer, please forward such written findings to our office for review. However, OCTC must defer to the decisions of the courts on these points. Accordingly, the facts you presented do not support an investigation into lawyer misconduct."**
- "For these reasons, the State Bar is closing this matter."
- "If you have new facts and circumstances that you believe may change our determination to close your complaint, you may submit a written statement with the new information to the Intake Unit for review. If you have any questions about this process, you may email Trial Counsel Lakshmi Jagannath at Lakshmi.Jagannath@calbar.ca.gov."
- "If you have presented all of the information that you wish to have considered, and you disagree with the decision to close your complaint, you may request that the State Bar’s Complaint Review Unit review your complaint. The Complaint Review Unit will recommend that your complaint be reopened if it determines that further investigation is warranted. To request review by the Complaint Review Unit, you must submit your request in writing, either:"
  - "1) Via email: Within 90 days of the date of this letter, by email to: CRU@calbar.ca.gov; or"
  - "2) Via United States Mail: Post-marked within 90 days of the date of this letter, by United States Mail to:" (The State Bar of California, Complaint Review Unit, Office of General Counsel, 180 Howard Street, San Francisco, CA 94105-1617)
