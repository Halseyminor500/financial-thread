§271(a) states as follows: / A Fam. C. §271 award of fees and costs based on culpable conduct in frustrating settlement and
unreasonably driving up litigation costs is “in the nature of the sanction.” / (Emphasis added). / Family Code §271 provides an
independent basis for sanctions in family law actions and may in fact authorize sanctions in response to reprehensible
conduct that does not rise to the level of sanctionable conduct under another statute. (See Marriage of Tharpe (2010) 188
Cal.App.4th 1295, 1318 – Family Code §271 “does not require that the sanctioned conduct be frivolous or taken solely for
delay”; Marriage of Freeman (2005) 132 Cal.App.4th 1, 6 – conduct sanctionable under §271 even if not “frivolous” for
purposes of CCP §907 appellate sanctions). / Family Code §271 sanctions still must be “tethered to attorney fees and costs.”
In other words, “sanctions available under the statute are limited to fees and costs,” and do not, for example, include
travel expenses necessarily incurred to attend court hearings or vacation time used for relief from work obligations. (See
*Menzes v. McDaniel* (2019) 44 Cal.App.5th 340, 350 – 351). / Family Code §271 also permits an award of attorney fees and
costs that have not yet been incurred but are “reasonably anticipated” to result from the offending individual’s conduct.
“[P]rovided there is adequate evidence that the awarded fees or costs will be incurred because of necessary actions [the
offended party] must take to remedy [the offending party’s] conduct, these expenses can be included in the Court’s award.”
(See *Id* at 348 – 350).”
