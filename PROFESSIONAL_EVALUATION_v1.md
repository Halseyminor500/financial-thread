## 1. THE STANDARD, IN THE WORDS THAT GOVERN (pointers to `LAW/`)

- **CRPC 3.3(a)(1):** a lawyer shall not "knowingly make a false statement of fact or law to a tribunal or fail to correct a
  false statement of material fact or law previously made to the tribunal by the lawyer." **3.3(a)(3):** shall not "offer
  evidence that the lawyer knows to be false." **1.0.1(f):** "knows" means "actual knowledge of the fact in question. A person's
  knowledge may be inferred from circumstances."
- **CRPC 3.4(f):** shall not "knowingly disobey an obligation under the rules of a tribunal". **Comment [2]:** "A violation of a
  civil or criminal discovery rule or statute does not by itself establish a violation of this rule."
- **CRPC 3.10(a):** "A lawyer shall not threaten to present criminal, administrative, or disciplinary charges to obtain an
  advantage in a civil dispute." **Comment [1]:** "a lawyer could not state or imply that a criminal or administrative action
  will be pursued unless the opposing party agrees to settle the civil dispute."
- **CRPC 4.1:** shall not "knowingly" make "a false statement of material fact or law to a third person".
- **CRPC 4.3(a):** with an unrepresented person whose interests conflict with the client's, "the lawyer shall not give legal
  advice to that person, except that the lawyer may, but is not required to, advise the person to secure counsel."
- **CRPC 3.2:** shall not "use means that have no substantial purpose other than to delay or prolong the proceeding or to cause
  needless expense."
- **CRPC 5.3(c)(2):** a lawyer with managerial or supervisory authority is responsible for a nonlawyer's conduct if the lawyer
  "knows of the conduct at a time when its consequences can be avoided or mitigated but fails to take reasonable remedial
  action."
- **BPC 6068(d):** "To employ, for the purpose of maintaining the causes confided to him or her, those means only as are
  consistent with truth, and never to seek to mislead the judge or any judicial officer by an artifice or false statement of
  fact or law." **6068(f):** "To advance no fact prejudicial to the honor or reputation of a party or witness, unless required
  by the justice of the cause".
- **FAM 2104(f):** the petitioner serves the PDD "within 60 days of filing the petition"; the time "may be extended by written
  agreement of the parties or by court order." **FAM 2107(c):** the court "shall" impose money sanctions on a noncomplying
  party "unless the court finds that the noncomplying party acted with substantial justification or that other circumstances
  make the imposition of the sanction unjust."
- **CCP 1010.6(c)(3):** an unrepresented party's express consent to electronic service is given by "Serving a notice on all
  parties and filing the notice with the court" or by "Manifesting affirmative consent through electronic means with the court
  or the court's electronic filing service provider". **CRC 2.251(c)(3)(B):** self-represented persons "are to be served by
  non-electronic methods unless they affirmatively consent to electronic service."
- **CCP 1013(a) and 1005(b):** mail service; periods extended by "20 calendar days if either the place of mailing or the place
  of address is outside the United States." **FAM 215(a):** after judgment, no later order "is valid unless any prior notice
  otherwise required to be given to a party … is served … upon the party. For the purposes of this section, service upon the
  attorney of record is not sufficient." **215(b):** a post-judgment motion to modify custody, visitation or child support
  "may be served … by first-class mail or airmail", with "an address verification" in the proof of service. **FAM 243(a):**
  for a DVRO request, "the respondent shall be personally served".
- **The Bar's intake test (2022 report):** complaints go to investigation if they "set out plausible, nonconclusory factual
  allegations that: (a) if true, would constitute a violation of the State Bar Act or Rules of Professional Conduct; or (b)
  suggest reasonable avenues of investigation that, if pursued, would be likely to develop facts constituting such a
  violation." **The 2026 closing letter (SP-0689):** "the State Bar needs specific facts which, if proved, would establish a
  violation"; "a disagreement between opposing litigants with respect to discovery or any challenges to service or a party's
  declaration does not mean that the lawyer committed misconduct … If that court were to find any impropriety by the lawyer,
  please forward such written findings to our office for review."
